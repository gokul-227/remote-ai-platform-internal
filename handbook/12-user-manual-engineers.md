# User Manual — Engineers

This is a step-by-step guide to using Remote AI Platform as an engineer looking for remote work. It
walks through the real screens and buttons in the product today — nothing here is aspirational.

## 1. Creating your account

Remote AI Platform doesn't use passwords. Go to the sign-up page and choose one of two ways in:

1. **Google, Microsoft, or GitHub** — click the matching button at the top of the page. You'll be sent to
   sign in with that account, then brought straight back into the platform, fully signed in. Nothing
   further to set up.
2. **Email code** — fill in your **Full name** and **Email address**, tick the box agreeing to the Terms
   of Service and Privacy Policy, then click **Continue**. On the next screen, tell the platform what
   you're here to do: click **"I am a Professional"** (the other option, "I am hiring / represent an
   organization," is for companies — see the Company Manual). Click **Send code** — Remote AI Platform
   emails you a one-time sign-in code (a short string of digits/letters). Enter it in the **Sign-in code**
   box and click **Verify & create account**. Didn't get it, or it expired? Click **Resend code** on the
   same screen.

That's it — there's no password to create, remember, reset, or leak. Every future sign-in works the same
way: go to the sign-in page, enter your email, click **Send code**, and enter the code Remote AI Platform
emails you (or just click Google/Microsoft/GitHub again if that's how you originally signed up).

> **If you're used to other products with a password field, note what's different here:** there is no
> "Forgot password?" link because there's no password to forget — a fresh email code every time you sign
> in serves the same purpose more simply. A GitHub *account* connection (used for one-click sign-in) is a
> separate thing from adding a GitHub *profile link* (your portfolio URL) later — the manual covers that
> separately below, under profile setup.

## 2. Setting up your profile (with AI, or by hand)

Right after your first sign-in, you'll land on the **Onboarding** wizard. You'll be asked to choose a
setup method:

- **AI Resume Import** (marked "Recommended") — click this tile, then click the upload area and choose
  your resume as a **PDF or DOCX file, up to 10MB**. The platform uploads it, then automatically runs it
  through an AI parser (you'll see "AI is extracting skills & experience...") that pulls out your:
  - Professional headline
  - Primary role
  - Location
  - Bio / summary
  - Skills
  - Years of experience

  You're then taken to a review screen with all of that pre-filled — check it over and correct anything
  the AI got wrong before continuing. If parsing fails for any reason, you can still continue and fill
  the form in by hand — nothing is lost.
- **Manual Setup** — skip the resume entirely and type in your headline, role, location, skills, years of
  experience, and bio yourself.

Either way, the last onboarding step asks for your **work preferences**: remote preference (100% Remote /
Hybrid / On-site), availability (Immediate / 2 Weeks / 1 Month / Exploring Opportunities), and your
**desired hourly rate**. Click **Complete Setup & View Dashboard** to finish.

You can come back and edit everything later from your **Profile** page (click **Edit** / the pencil
icon) — including adding project portfolio entries, education history, and links to your GitHub,
LinkedIn, and personal portfolio. Your profile page also shows a **completeness score** out of 100 and a
checklist of what's still missing (a bio, at least 3 skills, work experience, a project, a resume, and a
rate) — filling these in improves both how complete your profile looks to companies and how well you
match to jobs.

## 3. Browsing and searching jobs

Go to **Jobs** in the main navigation. From there you can:

- **Search** by keyword using the search bar.
- **Filter** by location, job type, experience level, "Remote only," and a minimum/maximum salary range.
- Use the quick **remote-region pills** (🌐 Remote, 🌍 EU Remote, 🗽 US Remote, 🇬🇧 UK Remote) for a fast
  filter.
- **Sort** results by Most Relevant, Newest First, or Salary (high-to-low or low-to-high).
- Click **popular skill** tags (React, TypeScript, Python, Go, Rust, AWS, etc.) to jump straight to jobs
  requiring that skill.

Jobs come from two places: companies posting directly on the platform, and an automated pipeline that
pulls in public remote listings from several external job boards (RemoteOK, Arbeitnow, Remotive,
USAJobs, and The Muse) roughly every six hours, so the list stays current even before many companies have
signed up directly.

## 4. Understanding your AI match score

Open any job listing and you'll see a **match score** specific to you and your profile. This isn't a
random number — it's computed from real, visible factors:

- **Skills overlap** (the biggest factor — 40% of the score) — how many of the job's required skills are
  on your profile.
- **Experience fit** (25%) — whether your years of experience meet what the role's seniority level
  typically needs.
- **Role fit** (15%) — how closely your primary role matches the job title.
- **Timezone fit** (8%) and **remote-work-preference fit** (2%) — whether the job's location/remote
  requirements line up with your stated preferences.
- **Availability** (7%) — whether you're currently marked as open to work.
- **Rate fit** (3%) — whether the job's budget covers your stated hourly rate.

Alongside the score, the platform shows a short, plain-language explanation of *why* you scored the way
you did — for example, calling out specific missing skills — so you're never guessing at a black-box
number.

## 5. Saving and applying to jobs

- To save a job for later without applying, click the **bookmark/save** icon on the job card or job
  detail page. Find everything you've saved from your Profile page's **Saved Jobs** link.
- To apply, open the job and click **Apply**. You can optionally add a short **cover note**. Once
  submitted, your application enters the pipeline: **Submitted → Reviewing → Shortlisted → Accepted** or
  **Rejected**. Track the status of every application you've sent from the **Applications** page.
- Changed your mind? As long as a company hasn't already accepted or rejected you, you can **withdraw**
  an application from the Applications page.
- Sometimes a company will **invite** you directly to a role instead of waiting for you to apply — this
  shows up in your Applications list as an invitation you can act on the same way.

## 6. Messaging

Once you're connected with a company or another member (see Network, below), you can message them
directly from the **Messages** page. Conversations update in real time — no need to refresh the page to
see a new reply.

## 7. The professional network and social feed

- **Network**: send and accept connection requests with other engineers and companies, and browse public
  engineer/company profiles.
- **Feed**: a LinkedIn-style social feed. Click **"Start a post, share an update…"** to write a post —
  choose from **Share an update**, **Project update**, **Celebrate achievement**, or **Write article** as
  the post type, set who can see it (Anyone / Connections only / Only me), and optionally attach an image
  or link URL. You can **Like**, **Comment**, **Repost** (UI only today — see note below), and **Share**
  (copies a link to the post) on anything in your feed.
- **Groups**: join or create interest-based groups and post inside them separately from the main feed.

One honest note: the **"Trending Skills"** panel you'll see in the feed's sidebar (and elsewhere) currently
just says **"Coming soon — we're still building this."** That's not a bug — the real feature (showing
which skills are actually rising in demand across the platform) hasn't been built yet, and the team chose
to say so plainly rather than show you fake numbers in the meantime.

## 8. Notifications

Click the notifications bell to see a live feed of updates: application status changes, new messages,
contract offers, and escrow/payment events (like a milestone being funded or released). Unread items are
badged with a live count, and you can mark items read individually or all at once.

## 9. Getting hired: contracts and payment

If a company decides to move forward with you, you'll receive a **contract offer** — a proposal covering
the rate, scope of work, and milestones. Review it from your **Contracts** page and **digitally sign** it
when you're ready. Once both you and the company have signed, the contract becomes **Active**.

From there:
- The company funds an **escrow payment** for a milestone — the money is held securely (via Stripe) but
  not released to you yet.
- You deliver the work and mark the milestone as delivered.
- Once the company approves it, they **release** the escrow — the funds move to your platform **wallet**.
- Your **Payments** page shows your wallet balance, how much is currently held in escrow, and your full
  transaction history.

This part of the product is genuinely functional today, not a mockup — but it's a lightweight system, not
a full accounting suite, so treat it as a convenient record and payment rail rather than a replacement for
your own invoicing/tax records.
